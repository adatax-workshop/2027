Call for Papers AdaTax • ICLR 2027

CALL FOR PAPERS

We invite work that explains, measures, or reduces the reliability cost of updating a foundation model. Empirical analyses, re-analyses of published methods on new model families, and negative results are explicitly welcome.

📝 Deadline 1 Feb 2027 (AoE)
📣 Notification 26 Feb 2027
📂 Venue OpenReview (link TBA)
The call opens on 1 December 2026 if the workshop is accepted. Details below are tentative until then.

Scope

Scope test. A submission is in scope if it explains, measures, or reduces the reliability cost of updating a foundation model. A paper that proposes a new adaptation method and reports only its gains is out of scope; the same paper with a measured cost is welcomed.

Updates include supervised fine-tuning, preference optimization, reinforcement learning, model merging, test-time adaptation, machine unlearning, and continual learning. Costs include losses in out-of-distribution generalization, calibration, robustness, safety, and retention of prior capabilities.

Topics of Interest

Including, but not limited to:

  • Mechanism: How supervised fine-tuning, preference optimization, reinforcement learning, model merging, test-time adaptation, machine unlearning, and continual learning change model parameters, representations, and behavior. This includes forgetting, feature distortion, calibration drift, and safety degradation; shared failure mechanisms across update methods; controlled comparisons of update methods at matched target-task improvement; and the effects of update data, including label shift, narrow-domain data, and model-generated data.
  • Measurement: Metrics and protocols for comparing reliability before and after an update, including OOD generalization, calibration and uncertainty estimation, selective prediction, OOD detection, safety compliance, and retention of prior capabilities. Also efficient indicators based on weights, activations, or behavior; label-free and sequential monitoring of test-time updates; update-aware benchmark design; cumulative costs of repeated or composed updates; and evaluation in healthcare, biomedicine, engineering, finance, and other high-stakes domains.
  • Mitigation: Methods that reduce adaptation costs while keeping the intended gains: constrained optimization, trust-region and KL-based objectives, feature preservation, model interpolation, parameter-efficient adaptation, and other methods that make the trade-off between gain and cost explicit. Also post-update repair, reliable model merging, stable and bounded test-time adaptation, abstention and rollback mechanisms, training choices that make later updates cheaper, and reporting standards for updated models.
  • Negative results and position papers: Mitigations that do not reduce the tax as expected; findings that do not hold across model families, tasks, or modalities; post-mortems of updates that degraded a deployed system; and arguments that the tax is overstated or an artifact of how we evaluate.

Submission Tracks

Both tracks are non-archival.

Track 1

Full papers

Up to 8 pages, excluding references and appendices. New methods, analyses, evaluations, or position arguments on the problems the workshop is built around.

Track 2

Short papers

Up to 4 pages, following ICLR's guidance for tiny and short papers and using the OpenReview venue template that ICLR provides. Priority goes to an implementation and evaluation of a simple unpublished idea, a modest self-contained theoretical result, a follow-up experiment or re-analysis of a published paper, or a fresh perspective on an existing publication.

The short track is aimed at authors outside the usual ML conference circuit, including domain scientists, practitioners, and first-time ICLR authors.

Policies

  • Non-archival. Accepted papers are public on OpenReview and imported into iclr.cc. There are no proceedings, and authors keep the right to publish the work elsewhere.
  • Concurrent submission to another venue is allowed when that venue permits it.
  • Previously published work. Work already published at an archival ML venue, including the ICLR 2027 main track, is not accepted in the full-paper track. A follow-up experiment or re-analysis of published work is welcome in the short track and is labeled as such.
  • Presentation. At least one author of each accepted paper presents in person. Authors who cannot travel for visa, health, or similar reasons may present remotely.

Review Process

Double-blind, on OpenReview.

How papers are reviewed

Each paper receives at least two reviews, and a third when the first two disagree; a non-conflicted organizer writes the decision. Reviewers answer four questions:

  • Does the paper state what the update cost and how that cost was measured?
  • Is the measurement sound, including baselines, variance across seeds, and checks for benchmark contamination?
  • Is the result informative, judged the same way whether it is negative or positive?
  • Is the paper clear?

How contributed work is featured

Every accepted paper presents a poster. Six contributed talks are chosen from the accepted papers by review score, with three constraints: all three questions are covered, at least one talk comes from the short track, and at least one presents a negative result. At equal quality, first-time ICLR presenters are chosen first.

Conflicts of interest

Organizers are not involved in assessing submissions from their own organization, current or former advisors or advisees, or co-authors of the past three years. Papers co-authored by an organizer may appear only as posters and are not eligible for awards or contributed talks.

AI Policy

Authors follow the ICLR 2027 AI Policy for Authors and reviewers the AI Policy for Reviewers. AI assistance in writing, coding, or analysis is allowed and must be disclosed. AI-generated papers are not accepted in either track; short-track submissions must be primarily human-authored. AI does not act as a primary author, reviewer, or meta-reviewer of workshop papers.
