Speakers AdaTax • ICLR 2027

MEET THE SPEAKERS

Our invited speakers come from the communities in which the adaptation tax has been documented: OOD detection and reliability, auditing, AI security and alignment, learning dynamics and continual learning, adaptation and unlearning, reinforcement learning, and biological foundation models.

Speakers have confirmed tentative interest. Talk titles and the panel are fixed after acceptance.

Invited Speakers

Letters refer to the three questions: (A) Mechanism, (B) Measurement, (C) Mitigation.

Razvan Pascanu
Google DeepMind • Mila

Proposed focus: Learning dynamics of forgetting and interference across update types A

Razvan Pascanu is a Research Scientist at Google DeepMind and an Affiliate Member at Mila. His research focuses on optimization and learning dynamics in deep networks, memory in recurrent models, and learning across multiple tasks, including continual, transfer, and meta-learning.

Sharon Li
University of Wisconsin–Madison

Proposed focus: What fine-tuning does to what a model can recognize as unfamiliar: OOD detection and reliability after updates AB

Sharon Li is an Associate Professor at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. Her research focuses on the foundations of safe and reliable AI systems, in both model development and deployment in the wild. She was named MIT Technology Review's Innovator of the Year in 2023 and a Sloan Research Fellow in 2025.

Yue Zhao
University of Southern California

Proposed focus: Detecting degradation without reference answers; auditing updated models and agents B

Yue Zhao is an Assistant Professor at the University of Southern California. His lab builds auditable AI systems, with work on anomaly and outlier detection at scale, LLM evaluation without reference answers, and the security and auditability of AI agents. He created the PyOD library for outlier detection, which is widely used in research and industry.

Maria Brbić
EPFL

Proposed focus: What transfers and what breaks when biological foundation models are adapted to new tissues, species, and assays B

Maria Brbić is an Assistant Professor at EPFL. She develops machine learning methods for complex and heterogeneous real-world data that generalize to settings never seen during training, with a focus on single-cell and molecular biology.

Bo Li
University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign

Proposed focus: Safety erosion under fine-tuning and merging, and what repair costs AC

Bo Li is an Associate Professor at the University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign. Her research focuses on AI security, trustworthy machine learning, and alignment, with an emphasis on robustness, security, privacy, generalization, and their connections. She received the IJCAI Computers and Thought Award, a Sloan Research Fellowship, and an NSF CAREER Award, and her group led the DecodingTrust evaluation of LLM trustworthiness.

Eleni Triantafillou
Google DeepMind

Proposed focus: Adaptation and unlearning that keep what should be kept C

Eleni Triantafillou is a Senior Research Scientist at Google DeepMind. Her research develops methods that let deep networks adapt efficiently to distribution shift, learn new tasks quickly, and unlearn specific data points.

Akshay Krishnamurthy
Microsoft Research

Proposed focus: Reinforcement-learning post-training: objectives, feedback, and what they preserve C

Akshay Krishnamurthy is a Senior Principal Research Manager at Microsoft Research. His research focuses on machine learning and statistics, with an emphasis on interactive learning and decision making with limited feedback, including contextual bandits, reinforcement learning, and their applications to language modeling and generative AI.

Aditi Raghunathan
Carnegie Mellon University

Proposed focus: Why fine-tuning distorts pretrained features, and predicting an updated model's out-of-distribution performance without labels AB

Aditi Raghunathan is an Assistant Professor at Carnegie Mellon University. Her research asks why frontier models fail and how to make them reliable and safe, combining theory with experiments at scale. She received her PhD from Stanford University in 2021, where her thesis won the Arthur Samuel Best Thesis Award, and holds a Schmidt Sciences AI2050 Early Career Fellowship.
